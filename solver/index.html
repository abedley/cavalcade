    How it searches

    A true brute force is out of reach. A game gives you 6 to 14 turns with up to 5 cards to choose from, which comes to around 108–109 different games, and Alice looks five plays ahead before each of her moves.

    So the solver runs a beam search in rounds. At each of your turns it tries every card from each position it is keeping, lets Alice reply as she would in the game, then keeps the positions where you lead by the most. The first round keeps 10 positions per turn, and each round keeps three times as many as the last. Wider rounds look at more of the game and usually find better lines, but take about three times as long. At the end it tries every pair of cards you could keep. The work is shared across your device’s processor cores.

    The result is the best line found so far, not a proven best. Leave it running for a better chance at the true best.
